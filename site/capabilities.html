Open problems

The full list of supported workloads will be published with the open-source code.

  1. Long-sequence attention. On Granite at 4,096 tokens, Latch's attention takes about 20 ms per update against about 14 ms for FlashAttention-2.
  2. Gradients of repeated gathers. Top-8 routing sends each token to eight experts. Latch’s deterministic backward scatter takes about 32 ms against about 5 ms conventionally. The number of contributions is known in advance. A direct sum per row may be faster.
  3. Fewer barrier-separated phases. Granite spends about 25 ms on 1,200 small elementwise, normalization and routing intervals. Standalone elementwise phases take 55% of E2-TTT’s memory-region time at four sequences. Combining these operations could reduce that cost.
  4. Smaller activation arenas. Latch saves activations in an arena without recomputation. E2-TTT’s published microbatch does not fit on an 80 GB H100.
  5. Feed-forward blocks remain 1.24–1.45× slower than compiled PyTorch.
  6. Faster lowering. This package cuts TinyMoE build time from 544 to 283 s on 64 vCPUs with identical output. Lowering still uses single-threaded Python and takes the largest share of most builds.
  7. Automatic build configuration. The compiler could choose device units, outlining and shared-memory reserves. These require explicit flags today.
  8. Large-batch efficiency. TinyMoE’s update speedup falls from 5.1× at 2 × 512 to 1.74× at 32 × 1024. An older measurement saved about 10% at the published batch of 16 × 8 × 1024. Granite remains slower at 4,096 tokens.
  9. Fused backward rotary unpack. rpackb gives wrong key-projection gradients when an elementwise gate sits between attention and its output projection. The skips-and-gates study found this defect. The rule needs a fix or a check that rejects the pattern.
  10. Numerical checks for BF16 expert models. Tested BF16 implementations of Granite differ from FP64 by a median 25–33% on individual accumulation microbatches at trained states. Latch and PyTorch also sometimes exceed the 0.08 gradient gate at TinyMoE’s trained 2 × 512 states. A separate study should establish a better-calibrated criterion before testing new candidates.
  11. New models and learning algorithms. Port another public model or test a learning rule that uses frequent small updates.
