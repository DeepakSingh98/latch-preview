Results

Latch generates GPU programs from models and learning rules. We test how fast they run and how much the compiler can handle.

Faster execution

Faster updates carried through to faster pretraining. The gains are larger when the work consists of many small operations or dependent learning steps.

Execution results on H100. Timings are Latch versus compiled PyTorch.
TaskWhat runsLatch vs PyTorch
Pretrain to the same quality TinyMoE-200M from scratch at batch 32 × 1,024. Same recipe and data; three paired seeds. 41–44% less update time16–17 vs 28–30 min to loss 3.30
Update weights at different rates TinyMoE shared weights update every microbatch; experts every 16. 6.5× faster execution10.28 vs 66.45 ms per microbatch
Train with small batches TinyMoE’s forward pass, loss, backward pass, clipping and AdamW. Batch 2 × 512. 5.1× faster updates12.57 vs 64.52 ms per update
Learn while reading GradLev adapts GPT-2’s final MLP. All 1,024 dependent updates run in one generated kernel. 3.5× faster adaptation53.98 vs 189.53 ms per block

H100 measurements from the preview release; times show Latch versus PyTorch. Each control runs the same learning procedure in compiled PyTorch; GradLev also uses CUDA graphs. Time to quality counts training updates, excluding compilation, evaluation and checkpointing.

Different models and learning rules

The compiler determines operation order, synchronization and memory reuse from the input program. We have also used it to generate training updates for:

Examples of different computation patterns handled by the compiler.
ModelWhat the compiler handles
Looped transformersRepeated layers with shared weights and gradients accumulated across visits.
State-space modelsSelective scans and recurrent state.
Diffusion transformersImage-denoising training with attention and feed-forward blocks.
Energy-based modelsSampling loops within a training update.

The looped transformer is in the preview package. The other three were tested in earlier releases.

Latch is a research preview with a limited set of supported operations. The goal is to make changing a learning program enough to produce a new GPU implementation. How the compiler works.
